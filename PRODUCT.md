## Platform

web

## Users

Confirmed by Josh, 2026-10-29. One site serves all of these:

- **Houston homeowners with clutter.** Garages, attics, spare rooms and old furniture they can't move themselves.
- **Move-outs and landlords.** Tenants moving out, and landlords or property managers clearing units.
- **People who listed something for free** and want it gone without waiting on strangers who never show.
- **Anyone who needs muscle.** Loading trucks and dumpsters, or moving heavy things, with no hauling needed.
- **Investors and partners.** The site is also proof of concept and proof that the business exists. Examples are subcontractor registration (AshBritt) and later financing or contracts.

Most visitors arrive on a phone.

## Product Purpose

The site wins the business its first paying pickup customers. The next month or two is about that one job (Josh, 2026-10-27). It shows the business is real and reachable. Success means a visitor texts, calls or emails a photo and asks for a free estimate.

## Positioning

The site makes two claims:

- **It takes small and odd jobs that bigger haulers don't want:**
  - single items
  - muscle-only help
  - picking up free-listed items
  - selling usable items for the customer
- **It's local, and eager to show it appreciates each customer's business.**

## Operating Context

The business is The Spring Cleaning Crew, a junk removal and hauling service in Houston, TX. Josh, the owner, does the physical work and runs the business from his phone between jobs.

How a customer gets a price:

1. The customer sends a photo.
2. They get a free estimate, which is a ballpark.
3. Josh sees the job in person and gives a firm quote. Nothing gets lifted before the customer says yes.
4. A deposit books haul-aways.

Usable items are resold or rehomed rather than dumped.

## Product Principles

1. **Truth first.** Every claim on the site is something Josh can stand behind today.
2. **Make reaching out effortless.** Texting a photo from a phone is the main action.
3. **No job too small.** Small, odd and one-off jobs are welcome, not tolerated.
4. **Show appreciation.** Every touchpoint should feel like a local business that's glad you called.
5. **Look like a real, going business.** The site has to convince investors and partners too.
